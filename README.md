# Postcards from my jungle

The Viridian Archive is a fictional jungle presented as a nocturnal field journal. Deep green foliage, warm paper, and soft photographic textures frame fifty imagined animal portraits. Visitors can browse the slideshow, reveal translations, and play sixteen creature calls. A separate Fernfox concept illustration introduces the world without being counted among the trained model’s fifty outputs.

I trained a small unconditional DDPM in Google Colab on real animal faces from Hugging Face’s AFHQ mirror. Four experiments compared 500 and 1,000 training images, 32- and 64-pixel resolution, and five versus two hundred epochs. RGB normalization, horizontal flips, fixed seeds, and a separate hundred-image holdout supported comparison. The initial twenty-epoch results were mostly noise, so training continued. The final run produced recognizable, mottled mammalian hybrids and reached a held-out noise prediction loss of approximately 0.058. This loss measures denoising performance, not aesthetic quality. Creature names and descriptions were assigned as imaginative interpretations after visual review.

For sound, I used the pre-trained AudioLDM small full v2 diffusion model. Sixteen distinct prompts describe chirps, croaks, clicks, purrs, and murmurs. The calls last between two and nine-and-a-half seconds. Each waveform receives level normalization and short boundary fades before WAV encoding. The language was generated with structured instructions to Codex, using eight shared roots and consistent three-word phrases. These invented phrases are poetic text; the nonverbal recordings do not pronounce them.

Image quality could improve with a broader licensed dataset containing whole animals, a larger network, higher resolution, and pretrained adaptation. The current face-heavy dataset limits anatomy and species variety. Audio could improve through careful listening, revised prompts, alternate seeds, and more inference steps.

AI assistance: the free version of OpenAI Codex assisted with code, notebooks, language, and documentation. Its built-in image generation tool created the separately labeled cover concept. The notebooks are cleaned reproduction workflows; actual experiment histories, prompts, seeds, and audio measurements are included alongside them.
